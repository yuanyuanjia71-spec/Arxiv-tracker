LoopAhead: Speculating Ahead for Faster Decoding in Looped Language Models
Authors: Zixuan Liu, Nan Xue, Rui Bao, Yuhang Xu, Xiaofeng Cao, Jiangchao Yao, Zhiyong Chen
First: 2026-10-15T09:38:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T09:38:11+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Looped Transformers improve parameter efficiency by repeatedly applying the same Transformer layers. However, generating each token still involves sequential computation and repeated access to model weights, leading to high decoding latency. Recent pipelined self-speculative decoding methods reduce this delay by drafting tokens from early-loop predictions and asynchronously advancing tokens at different recurrent depths in the same batch, but they do not fully exploit early-loop features for longer lookahead and still spend computation verifying incorrect speculative branches. We introduce \method, a speculative decoding framework for looped transformers that enables earlier drafting of future tokens and assigns complementary roles to different recurrent depths. A lightweight lookahead drafter (LAD) predicts multiple future tokens with early-loop states, and deeper-loop feedback helps decide which draft branches to continue and which to pause, while the full-depth model makes the final token decisions. LoopAhead achieves up to 7.82x decoding speedup over autoregressive decoding while preserving the target model's output distribution.

Looped Transformers improve parameter efficiency by repeatedly applying the same Transformer layers.
JetSpec++: Foresight Parallel Drafting with Self Refinements
Authors: Zhaoxiang Feng, Lanxiang Hu, Haoran Yuan, Yu-Yang Qian, Jihui Yang, Peng Zhao, Yibo Zhu, Tajana Rosing, Hao Zhang
First: 2026-10-14T08:37:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T06:05:59+00:00
Comments: 31 pages, 4 figures, 16 tables. Zhaoxiang Feng and Lanxiang Hu contributed equally. Code and models: https://github.com/hao-ai-lab/JetSpec
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Speculative decoding's effectiveness is governed by two quantities: the acceptance rate of drafted tokens and the cost of producing them. We show the two objectives can be co-optimized with algorithm-system co-design, and present JetSpec++, which retains a strong learned draft model while hiding its cost almost entirely. JetSpec++ combines three components: intra-block self refinement, which trains the draft model to iteratively correct its own parallel predictions within a block of tokens; inter-block foresight drafting, which begins the next block before the current one is verified; and pipelined drafting and verification, which overlaps the two stages so drafting's marginal wall-clock cost approaches zero. In comparison with state-of-the-art speculative decoding methods, JetSpec++ attains a wall-clock speedup of up to $7.45\times$ over autoregressive (AR) decoding and $1.54\times$ over DSpark, with up to 1,361 tokens per second for a single request on GB200, and an average acceptance length of 8.94 across seven benchmarks, while preserving the target model's output distribution. Our code and models are available at https://github.com/hao-ai-lab/JetSpec.

Speculative decoding's effectiveness is governed by two quantities: the acceptance rate of drafted tokens and the cost of producing them.
When Rotation Bounds Nothing: Streaming Image Classifiers to Field Nodes Under Physical Capture
Authors: Daniel Gilkarov, Ran Dubin
First: 2026-10-15T05:11:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T05:11:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Field nodes run image classifiers locally, exposing a first-class asset, the trained model, to theft. Trusted-execution enclaves, collaborative-inference splits, and encrypted storage assume the device owner is the adversary from deployment. Here the operator owns the node, and an adversary seizing it reads only what cold-boot capture finds resident. One answer stores nothing: a chunkable multi-exit model streams into volatile memory, rotating to bound residency. We characterise the capture envelope on real LTE traces across two victims of one architecture, against passive and adaptive adversaries. Neither survives a capable adversary; recovery cost alone differs. Against a from-scratch victim the uncaptured stages must be learned: the adaptive adversary gains most at shallow seizures, ends the CIFAR-10 envelope at every rotation, and stays rotation-bounded on CIFAR100, from 42.6% of usable seizures at 5 s to 86.8% at 30 s. Against the same architecture sliced from a public pretrained backbone, the adversary downloads those stages, recovering at least 90% of maximum accuracy at all depths, taking usable CIFAR-100 seizures from 0.0% to 97.3% at 5 s, given the victim's full labelled training set. Delivery-side secrecy raises a seizure's price; a public copy of the withheld stages erases it.

Field nodes run image classifiers locally, exposing a first-class asset, the trained model, to theft.
Long-Context Drafting Does Not Require Long-Context Drafters
Authors: Aref Jafari, Vansh Bhatia, Matt Qin, Subhajit Dutta Chowdhury, Ben Lee, Vikram Appia, Ali Ghodsi, Mehdi Rezagholizadeh
First: 2026-10-14T19:54:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T19:54:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Speculative decoding pairs a cheap drafter with parallel verification against the target. At ultra-long context it stops paying, because a drafter that attends over the full prefix inherits the target's context-dependent cost, so the speedup it was meant to provide first erodes and then reverses. The standard response is to build a long-context drafter, by training or fine-tuning one, or by using a multi-token-prediction head shipped inside the target checkpoint. We argue this is unnecessary, because the drafters in use are target-conditioned, reading hidden states the target computed over the entire prefix, so distant context already reaches them without their own attention, and verification corrects what they miss. We therefore freeze released short-context drafters and run them far past their training length under two inference-time mechanisms, one bounding the attention cache they see, which fixes their cost, and one re-basing their rotary position indices into the trained range. No weight is touched and the only tunable saturates early, so one vLLM-side configuration spans 4k to two million tokens, costing at most 0.16x of speedup at short length. We prove when a common index shift leaves the drafter's computation exactly unchanged, and decompose acceptance into two measurable gaps with sharp bounds. Whatever speedup a frozen drafter delivers at short context, it retains out to two million tokens, across three target-drafter pairs and five corpora. The claim concerns the flatness rather than the multiple, and removing either mechanism eliminates it. The gain is conditional on the speculative cost per emitted token staying below the matched target-only cost.
Summary / 总结
Speculative decoding pairs a cheap drafter with parallel verification against the target.
ReaDy: Recurrent States as Dynamic Context for Speculative Drafting in Hybrid Linear Attention Large Language Models
Authors: Bohan Sun, Zunhai Su, Xialie Zhuang, Jianjin Li, Ngai Wong, Chuan-Wei Kuo
First: 2026-10-14T17:08:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:08:52+00:00
Comments: 13 pages, 6 figures, 8 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive generation by drafting multiple tokens per step, yet feature-based drafters rely on target hidden features whose contextual emphasis may not align with the needs of subsequent draft positions. Hybrid linear attention (HLA) models maintain compact recurrent states that accumulate historical associations, offering an efficient source of complementary context for speculative drafting. We introduce ReaDy, the first speculative decoding method, to the best of our knowledge, to leverage HLA recurrent states for position-specific context retrieval during drafting. ReaDy equips each draft position with a learned query to retrieve supplementary context directly from the target's committed recurrent states. A lightweight state reader integrates this context into draft representations through residual updates, while low-rank state reads and fused operators keep additional computation and I/O overhead low. Building on DSpark's parallel backbone and sequential prediction head, ReaDy employs a lightweight two-stage adaptation strategy, with reader capacity, state sources, and residual placement examined through ablation studies. Across seven benchmarks spanning mathematics, code generation, and dialogue, ReaDy achieves an average $3.16\times$ decoding speedup over autoregressive decoding with Qwen3.8-27B on a single NVIDIA B200 GPU. It outperforms DSpark on every benchmark, improving throughput by 7.97% and acceptance length by 8.50% on average. Our code is available at https://github.com/StartLuxLabs/ReaDy.
Summary / 总结
Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive generation by drafting multiple tokens per step, yet feature-based drafters rely on target hidden features whose contextual emphasis may not align with the needs of subsequent draft positions.
FOLD: Position-Free Far Memory with Local Order for Long-Context Speculative Decoding
Authors: Shuxuan Li, Jialiang Kang, Yanghao Zhou, Ziye Zhu, Xiao Yang
First: 2026-10-14T15:55:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T15:55:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Speculative decoding accelerates LLM generation through lightweight drafting and parallel verification. It is particularly promising for long inputs, where KV-cache access costs can be amortized over multiple accepted tokens. Yet most drafters are trained on short sequences and degrade on long inputs, while long-context training is costly. Existing training-free approaches restrict the context window, rescale positions, or augment recent context with retrieved distant entries. Drafters, however, read target hidden states that already encode contextual and positional information, and their lightweight architecture makes full-history attention affordable. We propose FOLD, a training-free method that preserves exact local positions and treats all distant entries as far memory at a shared position. To counter attention dilution as the memory grows, we sharpen attention within it to emphasize relevant entries while separately controlling its total weight relative to the local window. Across six target-drafter pairs and diverse benchmarks of up to 256K tokens, FOLD predominantly delivers the highest speedup, with the best case more than doubling the speedup over autoregressive decoding, from 2.08$\times$ to 4.22$\times$.
Summary / 总结
Speculative decoding accelerates LLM generation through lightweight drafting and parallel verification.
TreeSpark: Learning to Draft the Trees You Verify for Speculative Decoding
Authors: Jihao Zhao, Qianyu Jiang, Shuaishuai Zu, Pengfei Li, Guanghuan Fang, Ben Feng, Biao Qin, Zhibin Wang, Baochuan Yang
First: 2026-10-14T13:21:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T13:21:09+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parallel drafters such as DFlash and DSpark reduce proposal latency, but early mismatches limit how much of each block can be accepted. Retaining alternatives introduces parent states and candidate choices that reference-conditioned training does not directly supervise. We introduce TreeSpark, a unified training and inference framework extending the semi-autoregressive DSpark architecture. It constructs parent-conditioned candidate trees from one backbone pass and replays their expansion, pruning, and budgeted packing during training. Survival negative log-likelihood weights retained edges by reference-derived path mass, while a capped coverage hinge promotes target-important alternatives near local selection boundaries. Both objectives reuse reference-chain target distributions without additional target forward passes. We evaluate greedy decoding with Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B on nine general benchmarks and six writing domains. Across targets and suites, TreeSpark improves mean acceptance length over DSpark by 23.4% to 31.6% and mean single-request throughput by 15.1% to 25.0%. Batched Qwen3-4B throughput improves by 12.6% to 17.2%. At fixed attention capacity and inference policy, the proposed supervision further improves mean acceptance length by approximately 6.4% and mean system throughput by 4.2% to 5.0% on Qwen3-4B.
Summary / 总结
Parallel drafters such as DFlash and DSpark reduce proposal latency, but early mismatches limit how much of each block can be accepted.
JointServe: Serving Multi-Model Agents on Memory-Constrained GPUs
Authors: Daijin Hu
First: 2026-10-14T08:59:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:59:35+00:00
Comments: 18 pages, 12 figures, 1 table
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LLM agents are evolving from a single model calling tools to planners that orchestrate multiple expert models whose capabilities live in distinct weights. This shift breaks a hidden assumption of today's serving systems: that the working set of models fits in GPU memory. On the memory-constrained GPUs typical of private and edge deployments, naive whole-model swapping serializes weight transfer with computation and, without host-backed KV restoration, discards session state at a switch. We present JointServe, a serving system that treats weight shards and KV blocks as residency objects competing for one unified memory budget. JointServe exploits three structural properties of agent workloads (directed calls, gapped execution, and dual-state residency) to prepare models before they are called: an intent signal parsed from the caller's own output stream triggers prefetch during generation, partial k* residency reduces switching to incremental layers, and a preemptive transfer scheduler protects live-session KV with a guaranteed bandwidth share. A carve-out control step adjusts the weight/KV allocation as workload demands change. Evaluations show that JointServe reduces session completion time by 21.7% over the strongest whole-model swap baseline and stays usable in a crash regime where every whole-model swap baseline fails to finish (a 46-fold gap). On replayed SWE-bench, tau-bench, and OSWorld traces, it cuts session completion time by up to 49% and lifts SLO attainment from 2.8-24.5% to 76.8-100%.
Summary / 总结
LLM agents are evolving from a single model calling tools to planners that orchestrate multiple expert models whose capabilities live in distinct weights.
New Model, Old Habits: Behavior Transfer under Model Swapping in Multi-Session LLM Agents
Authors: Youngbin Choi, Sehyun Hwang, Suhyeon Jun, Seoyeon Lee, Sunghyun Choi, MoonJeong Park, Dongwoo Kim
First: 2026-10-14T07:38:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T07:38:54+00:00
Comments: 27 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multi-session agents can switch between LLMs to balance performance and inference cost. Such switches require transferring a history for continuity, yet their effects on the recipient remain underexplored. Through controlled experiments with multi-session tasks, we uncover behavior transfer: recipients tend to adopt reasoning and action patterns from the models that generated their histories. This effect is most evident with raw-history transfer but is substantially reduced by summarization. Based on these findings, we propose Direction- and Authorship-aware Summarization of History (DASH) that preserves stronger-model behavior while minimizing weak-to-strong transfer via summarization. Experiments with repeated swaps show that DASH outperforms raw-history transfer and naive summarization baselines. These results suggest that effective multi-model orchestration requires considering not only which model to use, but also which behavioral patterns to carry forward.

Multi-session agents can switch between LLMs to balance performance and inference cost.
SuperAI: Merging Capabilities Across Heterogeneous Foundation Models
Authors: Jingtong Dou, Chuancheng Shi, Wenhua Wu, Wei Bao, Fei Shen, Zhiyong Wang, Tat-Seng Chua
First: 2026-10-11T03:51:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T07:18:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Enabling the reuse and composition of knowledge and capabilities distributed across foundation models is an important step toward building scalable artificial intelligence (AI) systems. However, architectural differences and incompatible representations make it difficult to transfer and integrate these capabilities through a unified cross-model interface. To address this challenge, we propose SuperAI, a cross-architecture multi-model fusion framework that integrates complementary capabilities from multiple source models into a host model through orthogonal alignment of low-dimensional task-relevant representations. Specifically, we first extract task-relevant representations from the source models and the host model using shared anchor samples, and apply principal component analysis (PCA) to construct low-dimensional functional subspaces with the same dimensionality. Next, we use orthogonal Procrustes mappings to align the source and host coordinate systems, establishing cross-model correspondences while preserving the internal geometry of each source subspace. Finally, we train a lightweight residual multilayer perceptron (MLP) with joint supervision on task outputs and source-model increments, learning input-dependent residual increments that are injected into the host model for multi-model capability fusion. Experiments on three host models across 15 capability domains show that SuperAI achieves higher average domain scores than the evaluated cross-architecture fusion methods and better preserves general capabilities as the number of source models increases. Ablation studies further support the effectiveness of orthogonal alignment, nonlinear residual mapping, and joint supervision.
Summary / 总结
Enabling the reuse and composition of knowledge and capabilities distributed across foundation models is an important step toward building scalable artificial intelligence (AI) systems.
