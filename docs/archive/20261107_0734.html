Edge-Assisted Crowdsensing for Explainable Cyclist Safety Perception
Authors: Hongsen Cao, Chia-Yen Chiang, Mona Jaber, Meihui Wang, Yasmin Fathy
First: 2026-11-05T18:33:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:33:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cyclist fatality rates remain high despite policy support for active travel, and autonomous vehicles add complexity to mixed traffic. Neither crash statistics nor multi-model detection pipelines scale to continuous monitoring of perceived safety along a route. We present an edge-assisted crowdsensing system: handlebar cameras supply street-level frames, a vision-language model (VLM) turns each frame into structured traffic attributes through a single prompt, and an explainable boosting machine (EBM) combines them with OpenStreetMap road infrastructure and on-bike kinematics to predict a crowdsourced 1-9 safety score. Extraction belongs at the network edge: the fastest self-hosted model needs 2.49 s per frame and scoring takes 0.4 ms on a CPU. On 1,362 annotated frames from two London routes, the EBM regressor reaches 72.2 percent binary accuracy and a mean absolute error of 1.011 under five-fold cross-validation. It is within one accuracy point of black-box ensembles. Its per-feature shape functions give exact attribution: physical lane separation is worth +0.97 rating points, a painted lane +0.70 and a construction site -0.65. Planners can therefore price an infrastructure change in rating points, and the results enter cost-benefit analysis directly. We also report inter-annotator agreement (alpha = 0.4695), an attribute audit, and a sensitivity analysis linking attribute error to accuracy. With re-encoding, a 20 s sampling interval serves 1,000 riders at 54 Mbit/s at a cost of 1.4 accuracy points, making continuous monitoring practical.

ValuSpec: Plug-and-Play Candidate Valuation before Target Verification for Tree-Based Speculative Decoding
Authors: Liang He, SiYuan Ma, Qishi Zhan, Yongqi Fan, Mingyu Cao, Qizhen Lan, Zhaolu Kang, Xilu Wang
Venue: NeurIPS 2026 poster
First: 2026-10-22T14:23:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T15:09:32+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026 poster
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Tree-based speculative decoding accelerates large language model inference by verifying multiple candidate branches in parallel. However, in practical serving scenarios, similar requests repeatedly induce overlapping candidate branches. While retrieval-based methods mitigate this by reusing historical drafts, the resulting candidate trees often contain many low-quality branches as the candidate tree grows, which reduces verification efficiency and wastes target-side computation. To address this, we propose ValuSpec, a plug-and-play candidate valuation mechanism that filters low-quality branches from a supplied candidate tree before expensive target-side verification. ValuSpec introduces transfer-based supervision to align filtering with the target model's behavior. A filtering threshold is estimated from the target model's greedy trajectories on a calibration split, without any parameter training. The target model then verifies the filtered tree under the output-identical greedy rule, so accepted tokens are guaranteed to match standard autoregressive decoding exactly. Experimental results on HumanEval, GSM8K, Dolly, and SpecBench show that, in our main evaluation, ValuSpec achieves up to 2.38x end-to-end speedup in retrieval-based tree settings. Furthermore, integrating ValuSpec into the EAGLE-3 dynamic tree framework yields up to 2.78x end-to-end speedup without modifying its tree construction strategy, demonstrating that ValuSpec serves as a generally applicable and plug-and-play candidate valuation module.

Tree-based speculative decoding accelerates large language model inference by verifying multiple candidate branches in parallel.
KVBuffer: IO-aware Serving for Linear Attention
Authors: Longwei Zou, Lin Zhong
First: 2026-05-18T19:14:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T15:09:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Linear attention has recently gained significant attention for long-context inference due to its constant decoding cost with respect to context length. However, existing serving systems typically serve linear attention by recurrently computing and updating a large linear attention state in every decoding step. Since the state is much larger than the per-token key and value, recurrent decoding incurs substantial memory access and becomes inefficient for serving linear attention. In this paper, we propose KVBuffer, an IO-aware serving mechanism for linear attention. By buffering recent keys and values, KVBuffer enables serving systems to compute linear attention outputs in more flexible and memory-efficient ways. For decoding, KVBuffer enables chunkwise computation, which reduces average memory access and decoding latency by deferring state updates and applying them in batch. For speculative decoding, KVBuffer verifies draft tokens in parallel and avoids storing temporary states. For short contexts, KVBuffer computes attention outputs directly from buffered keys and values, without creating or updating the linear attention state. We implement KVBuffer in SGLang for Qwen3.5 and Nemotron-3 series models. Our evaluations show that KVBuffer can speedup batch decoding by up to 1.47x and increase the maximum number of serving requests by 5x for speculative decoding when verifying four draft tokens.

Linear attention has recently gained significant attention for long-context inference due to its constant decoding cost with respect to context length.
FlashOffloading: Low-Latency KV Cache Offloading for Long-Context LLM Serving
Authors: Xuan Wang, Zhuanhao Wu, Tingzhu Fang, Xushi Li, Sicheng Zhang, Zhenyu Yang, Feng Liu, Zhaohui Fan, Qiang Xie, Huayi Zou, Yuncong Liu, Zeyu Song, Ke Zhu, Yanjia Zeng, Jingchun Gao, Yan Shi, Kui Luo, Gezi Li, Liangjun Feng, Cheng Li

Comments: 13 pages including references,13 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
In large language model (LLM) serving systems, the key-value (KV) cache grows linearly with sequence length and concurrency, so long-context inference easily hits the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) capacity wall and results in low concurrency. Offloading the KV cache to host dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) frees HBM space and raises concurrency, but the additional cache management and host-to-device (H2D) load overhead inflates inference latency, higher concurrency fails to translate into effective throughput. We present FlashOffloading, a host-DRAM-centric inference architecture that fully offloads the KV cache in both the prefill and decode phases. FlashOffloading builds rolling HBM cache for prefill, a hot cache for decode, a reverse sparse index with zero extra miss-lookup overhead, and a compute--load overlapped kernel, optimizing KV offloading latency across the entire pipeline and delivering throughput gains under a time-per-output-token (TPOT) service-level objective (SLO). In experiments deploying GLM-5.2 with multi-token prediction (MTP3) speculative decoding on an Ascend910C prefill--decode (PD)-disaggregated platform, FlashOffloading improves prefill throughput by up to 1.38\(\times\) and decode throughput by up to 1.91\(\times\) under a 30 ms TPOT constraint. FlashOffloading is integrated into an open-source serving framework: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm-ascend/tree/releases/v0.30.0.

ReviewMap: Mapping Evaluative Acts and Automation Boundaries in Peer-Review Comments
Authors: Kihyun Na, Injung Kim
First: 2026-11-05T05:49:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T05:49:01+00:00
Comments: 27 pages, 5 figures, 19 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Peer-review comments check what a paper does and judge why it matters, but no existing scheme distinguishes these two evaluative acts. We argue that making these acts explicit and measuring how reliably LLMs identify them is a prerequisite for responsible LLM-assisted review analysis: humans need to inspect review judgments and their grounds. This defines a more honest mission for NLP in peer review than full automation. We present ReviewMap, a framework that classifies each comment as assessment of claims and scholarly standards or calibration of contribution value and venue fit, with finer-grained attributes applied conditionally by act type. Applied to 500 ICLR~2026 papers through multi-model LLM consensus, the act layer proves reliably automatable under the operational guideline (inter-model $κ\geq 0.75$), supported by five-human validation on 470 development comments, while the attribute layer requires human oversight, defining a measured automation boundary. At the corpus level, 17% of reviewer-paper instances contain no panel-coded calibration, and the panel-coded calibration share is nearly $3\times$ as high in Strengths as in Weaknesses. This boundary informs three affordances: a review guideline that operationalizes the assessment-then-calibration workflow for reviewers, a per-comment rebuttal strategy scaffold for authors, and a discussion-phase dashboard for area chairs.

HyFlash: A Prefix-Memory Prior for On-Device Speculative Decoding
Authors: Jongwoo Ko, Pashmina Cameron
First: 2026-11-05T00:31:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T00:31:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parallel block drafting accelerates speculative decoding by predicting multiple future positions in a single backbone pass, but on-device deployment constrains both drafter capacity and context storage. Under tight memory budgets, drafters must model long-range dependencies and fine-grained draft-position interactions without the growing KV-cache overhead of full-context attention. We propose HyFlash, a compact parallel drafter that introduces a prefix-memory prior to separate long-range context modeling from fine-grained draft-position interactions with limited capacity. Local attention captures recent context and draft-position interactions, while recurrent memory compresses historical target features into fixed-size states. This inductive bias enables effective local and long-range modeling with bounded draft-side memory. Experiments show that HyFlash achieves higher acceptance than full-context attention with substantially less KV storage and with larger gains for shallow drafters and long contexts.

FastAFD: When Does Attention-FFN Disaggregation Pay on Rack-Scale NVLink?
Authors: Yichao Fu, Yuxuan Zhang, Minshen Zhang, Ruitian Wang, Junda Chen, Hao Zhang
First: 2026-11-04T21:21:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T21:21:57+00:00
Comments: 24 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Attention-FFN disaggregation (AFD) separates attention from the experts of a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model and has been reported to raise decode throughput over colocated serving. Whether this advantage holds on rack-scale NVLink systems such as GB200 NVL72 remains unclear: prior results were obtained on other hardware, against narrower colocated baselines, or with analytical models, whereas production engines serve large MoE models on these systems with wide expert parallelism (wide EP). We build and tune FastAFD, an AFD engine for GB200 NVL72 that overlaps the MoE of one microbatch with the attention of the other, and compare it against the best vLLM wide EP setting. Experiments show that AFD has a throughput advantage of up to 1.17x over wide EP on Qwen3 and 1.16x on Qwen3.5, and matches it on MiniMax-M2.5. We derive an analytical model that predicts AFD throughput within 3% using only profiles of colocated vLLM, telling whether switching to AFD would pay off before deploying it. The model yields a concise insight: AFD's speedup factors into a batch gain (larger batches, since attention GPUs hold no expert weights), an overlap gain (shorter steps, since the MoE runs on the expert GPUs behind attention), and a disaggregation cost (the GPUs given to experts). AFD has the advantage when the two gains outweigh the cost. This view explains which models benefit from AFD, and shows that speculative decoding helps light-expert models most, where the enlarged overlap gain outweighs the larger disaggregation cost and adds a further 8% to AFD's advantage over wide EP. Looking ahead, serving the experts on accelerators that keep their weights in on-chip SRAM, such as the Groq LPX accelerators paired with Vera Rubin NVL72, would remove most of the disaggregation cost, raising the ideal speedup to 2.48x with speculative decoding. FastAFD is open source at https://github.com/hao-ai-lab/FastAFD.
Summary / 总结
Attention-FFN disaggregation (AFD) separates attention from the experts of a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model and has been reported to raise decode throughput over colocated serving.
Teaching Diffusion to Speculate Left-to-Right
Authors: Lexington Whalen, Yuki Ito, Ryo Sakamoto
First: 2026-06-10T01:21:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T18:51:59+00:00
Comments: 13 pages, technical report
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) achieve remarkable performance across a wide range of tasks, but their autoregressive decoding process incurs substantial inference costs due to inherently sequential token generation. Speculative decoding addresses this bottleneck by employing a lightweight draft model to propose multiple future tokens that are subsequently verified in parallel by a larger target model. Recent work has demonstrated that diffusion language models are well suited for this setting, as they can generate entire blocks of draft tokens in parallel and thereby alleviate the sequential constraints of autoregressive drafting. A subtlety of this regime is that block-diffusion drafters generate tokens bidirectionally within a block, whereas verification is performed by an autoregressive target model that evaluates tokens in a strictly left-to-right manner, leaving a gap between the symmetric training-time objective and the asymmetric verification-time reward. In this work, we offer an empirical analysis of three training-time interventions that narrow this gap: token positional weighting, a first-error focal loss that targets the position that breaks the accepted prefix within each block, and a chain loss term that substitutes a differentiable surrogate for the expected accepted length. The three interventions act along orthogonal axes (position, block-conditional first error, joint prefix) and compose additively; they are likewise orthogonal to test-time alignment mechanisms such as multi-draft self-selection, with which they can in principle be combined. Across four target models and six reasoning, code, and dialogue benchmarks, the three interventions raise accepted draft length by 21-76% per benchmark over a position-uniform baseline, without adding additional forward passes and without changing the inference pipeline or the rejection-sampling exactness contract.
Summary / 总结
Large language models (LLMs) achieve remarkable performance across a wide range of tasks, but their autoregressive decoding process incurs substantial inference costs due to inherently sequential token generation.
D3D: Decoupled Diffusion Drafting for Asynchronous Speculative Decoding
Authors: Baoyou Chen, Ruiqiao Mei, Guancong Lin, Tao Song, Weihao Yuan, Qun Liu, Siyu Zhu
First: 2026-11-04T12:27:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T12:27:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Target-conditioned diffusion drafters predict candidate tokens in parallel, yet each new block must wait for updated target-model hidden states. This forces drafting and verification to run sequentially, limiting speedup even though each block is generated in one pass. We introduce D3D, a standalone diffusion drafter that removes this inter-round dependency. D3D combines blockwise denoising with auxiliary autoregressive supervision to achieve long accepted prefixes without target hidden states. This independence lets D3D draft successor blocks during verification in one packed pass, enabling asynchronous speculative decoding. We progressively prune layers according to their effect on acceptance length to reduce the cost of packed drafting. Removing five of 36 layers retains over 99\% of mean acceptance length across three benchmarks with Qwen3-8B as the target. Across seven benchmarks, D3D averages 5.00$\times$ and 5.71$\times$ decoding speedup with Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B targets, compared with 4.03$\times$ and 3.67$\times$ for DFlash. On GSM8K with Qwen3-32B, speedup reaches 7.48$\times$. Code is publicly available at https://github.com/fudan-generative-vision/D3D.

Split-KV: Efficient Context Parallelism for Training Diffusion Drafters
Authors: Geng Zhang, Fangzhao Zhang, Cong Xie, Tiantian Fan, Yang You
First: 2026-11-04T04:16:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T04:16:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Diffusion drafters accelerate speculative decoding by denoising multiple draft tokens in parallel. Their training uses anchor sampling to construct compact draft queries and performs cross-attention with a block-sparse mask over a much longer context. We empirically show that acceptance length remains stable as context scales with a fixed query budget, while increasing the query budget yields diminishing quality gains, jointly establishing a practical regime with queries far shorter than the context. No existing context parallelism (CP) method jointly exploits this query--context asymmetry and meets the nonlinear, sample-dependent load-balancing requirement created by anchor sampling. We present Split-KV, an exact CP method that keeps key/value shards local and limits per-layer attention communication to query-sized tensors, making this communication independent of context length. Its global query view further enables a cost-aware longest-processing-time-first (LPT) strategy that balances context key/value chunks by their realized attention cost. At a CP size of 64, Split-KV retains 85--91\% weak-scaling efficiency and achieves up to $3.67\times$ speedup over the fastest available CP baseline. LPT achieves $<$1\% imbalance and plans three orders of magnitude faster than the best load-balancing baseline. Code is available at https://github.com/zxgx/verl.
Summary / 总结
Diffusion drafters accelerate speculative decoding by denoising multiple draft tokens in parallel.
Online Draft Co-Training for Speculative Decoding in Large-Scale, Long-Context RL Post-Training
Authors: Zili Wang, Zhaopeng Qiu, Yuekai Zhang, Shuang Yu, Seonjin Na, Hayate Iso, Junjie Lai
First: 2026-09-07T06:48:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T03:48:05+00:00
Comments: Technical Report
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Speculative decoding accelerates rollout generation, which dominates the cost of reinforcement learning (RL) post-training. Online co-training keeps the draft aligned with the evolving policy, further increasing acceptance length and speedup. However, training large models on long contexts requires context parallelism (CP) and pipeline parallelism (PP), and co-training drafts under this layout poses two obstacles: (1) the draft's branch attention is unsupported by causal CP, and (2) the target features that the draft consumes are spread across PP stages. In this paper, we present the design and implementation of online draft co-training in NeMo-RL. For CP, we extend packed, load-balanced zigzag ring attention by merging rank-local branch attention with causal main-sequence attention. For PP, TapChannel transports intermediate target features across stages via a separate path, leaving the pipeline schedule unaffected. Experiments show that co-training keeps the policy's reward and accuracy close to the no-draft baseline while delivering $1.48$--$2.25\times$ rollout speedups and $1.19$--$1.88\times$ end-to-end training speedups across model scales up to 122B. Our CP design achieves strong scaling at 256K sequence length with significant memory savings over prior work, and our PP transport incurs modest overhead. Code can be found at https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/RL/issues/3698.

RECAP: Context-Aware Prediction Recycling with Request-Local and Persistent Memories for Multimodal Speculative Decoding
Authors: Hengjie Zhu, Dayan Wu, Xinze Liu, Peng Fu, Zheng Lin, Weiping Wang
First: 2026-11-04T03:32:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T03:32:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Recycling a target model's own predictions offers a training-free source of proposals for speculative decoding. In vision-language models, however, existing recycling indexes predictions by the last token alone, conflating predictions computed under different images and prefixes; we find that such reuse fails most often at image-sensitive decoding states. Longer keys reduce this aliasing but match fewer states. We introduce \method, which stores target predictions under one-, two-, and three-token keys: contextual memories live only for the current request and are thus implicitly conditioned on its image, while token-level memory persists across requests to retain coverage. At each lookup, entropy-confidence weighting fuses the retrieved predictions according to their key specificity and concentration. Best-first search composes the fused transitions into a candidate tree, supplemented by suffix-matched continuations, and one target pass verifies the tree under greedy decoding. Without training or an auxiliary draft model, \method\ achieves mean speedups over autoregressive decoding of $1.66\speed$ and $1.56\speed$ across seven single-turn benchmarks on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.6-7B, exceeding the strongest training-free baseline, Token Recycling ($1.51\speed$ and $1.37\speed$). Our implementation is available in https://github.com/Senmo996/RECAP-official-code.

Recycling a target model's own predictions offers a training-free source of proposals for speculative decoding.
